TV Series Analysis

Quality vs. Popularity | DSA210 Term Project by Kamil

1. Overview & Motivation

The main goal of this project is to investigate the relationship between the quality of a TV series and its mainstream popularity. It is often assumed that high-quality content automatically attracts a larger audience. I tested this assumption using real data by analyzing whether a higher IMDb rating correlates with a larger audience size (number of votes).

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

The ratings show a nearly normal distribution centered around 7.5 to 8.5, indicating a high baseline quality for the selected series. However, the audience size distribution is heavily right-skewed, meaning that while most shows have a moderate audience, a few mega-hits pull in millions of votes.

Figure 1: Scatter plot showing the weak linear relationship between IMDb ratings and total user votes.
Figure 2: Distribution histograms of ratings and votes alongside the overall correlation heatmap.

3. Hypothesis Testing

  • Rating vs. Audience Size (Pearson Correlation): Output is 0.30 (p-value = 0.000). There is a statistically significant but weak positive correlation. High ratings help, but do not guarantee mass popularity.
  • Classic vs. Modern Series (T-Test): The p-value is 0.005. Older shows (< 2017) have a significantly higher average rating (8.17) than modern shows (7.94).

4. Machine Learning (K-Means Clustering)

Using unsupervised learning, I segmented the TV landscape into 4 distinct profiles. The Elbow Method indicated that k=4 was the optimal number of clusters.

Figure 4: Elbow method metric plot used to determine the optimal number of clusters (k=4).

The algorithm successfully divided the TV series into 4 distinct real-world profiles:

  • Cluster 3 (The Mega-Phenomena): E.g., Game of Thrones. Exceptional quality and massive votes.
  • Cluster 0 (Mainstream Masterpieces): E.g., The Boys. High-quality, widely watched series.
  • Cluster 2 (Hidden Gems): E.g., Succession. Critically acclaimed but smaller, niche audiences.
  • Cluster 1 (Popcorn Entertainment): E.g., The Flash. High popularity but average critical ratings.
Figure 5: Scatter plot representing the final 4-cluster model output.

5. Conclusion

This project successfully demonstrated how unsupervised machine learning and statistical testing apply to the entertainment industry. The most fascinating finding was how the K-Means algorithm naturally discovered the "Hidden Gems" and "Popcorn Entertainment" categories purely from raw numerical data.
