TV Series Analysis: Quality vs. Popularity

A statistical and machine learning exploration deciphering if critical acclaim directly converts to global mass-market audience volume.

Scroll Down

1. Overview & Motivation

The main goal of this project is to investigate the relationship between the quality of a TV series and its mainstream popularity. It is often assumed that high-quality content automatically attracts a larger audience. I tested this assumption using real data by analyzing whether a higher IMDb rating correlates with a larger audience size (number of votes).

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

The ratings show a nearly normal distribution centered around 7.5 to 8.5, indicating a high baseline quality for the selected series. However, the audience size distribution is heavily right-skewed, meaning that while most shows have a moderate audience, a few mega-hits pull in millions of votes as global anomalies.

Figure 1: Scatter plot showing the weak linear relationship between IMDb ratings and total user votes.
Figure 2: Distribution histograms of ratings and votes alongside the overall correlation heatmap.

3. Hypothesis Testing

To confirm visual trends with pure mathematical certainty, I deployed three primary statistical computations:

  • Rating vs. Audience Size (Pearson Correlation): Output is 0.30 (p-value = 0.000). There is a statistically significant but weak positive correlation. High ratings help, but do not guarantee mass popularity.
  • Classic vs. Modern Quality (Independent T-Test): The p-value is 0.005. Older shows (< 2017) have a significantly higher average rating (8.17) than modern series (7.94).
  • Classic vs. Modern Popularity (Mann-Whitney U Test): Output is p-value = 0.012. Modern series (2017 and newer) possess a statistically significant larger audience volume (numVotes) compared to classic shows, reflecting the global streaming boom.

4. Machine Learning (K-Means Clustering)

Using unsupervised machine learning, I segmented the TV landscape into 4 distinct profiles. The Elbow Method indicated that k=4 was the optimal number of clusters for this feature space.

Figure 4: Elbow method metric plot used to determine the optimal number of clusters (k=4).

The algorithm successfully divided the dataset into 4 distinct real-world ecosystem profiles:

Cluster 3: Mega Phenomena

Exceptional technical quality coupled with massive, outlying vote counts. (e.g., Game of Thrones, Breaking Bad)

Cluster 0: Mainstream Hits

High-quality, widely watched series comfortably crossing the standard popularity thresholds. (e.g., The Boys, Sherlock)

Cluster 2: Hidden Gems

Critically acclaimed masterpieces boasting elite ratings, but watched by smaller, highly niche audiences. (e.g., Succession, Severance)

Cluster 1: Popcorn Entertainment

Shows securing massive commercial popularity and viewership despite lower or average critical reviews. (e.g., The Flash, Arrow)

Figure 5: Multi-dimensional scatter plot representing the final 4-cluster machine learning output.

5. Conclusion & Reflections

This project successfully applied a combination of statistical validation and machine learning to map the relationship between critical quality and mainstream popularity in television.

Core Takeaways:

  • Correlation is Not Causation: With a weak 0.30 correlation, we have proven that while a great rating (9.0+) helps, it is not the single driver of popularity. Marketing, accessibility, and genre likely play massive roles.
  • The "Hidden Gems" Segment: The algorithmic discovery of Cluster 2 (high rating, lower audience) mathematically validates the existence of prestige niche shows that are beloved by critics but do not achieve mainstream dominance.
  • The Decline of Newer Series: The T-Test outcome confirming that older series have significantly higher average ratings suggests a potential shifting standard in both television production and user rating behavior over time.

Ultimately, this investigation shows that the TV landscape is far more nuanced than "good equals popular." This has implications for streaming platforms (how they recommend), creators (defining success beyond raw numbers), and audiences (discovering quality outside the mainstream anomalies).

Future Directions: While successful, this project could be expanded by integrating additional data like **streaming hours**, **marketing budget data**, and **detailed genre analysis** to further decipher what drives a show from being a critically acclaimed "Hidden Gem" into a global "Mega Phenomenon."
